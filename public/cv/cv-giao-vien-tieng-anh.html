Teaching Philosophy

I teach English as something children use, not something they store. Montessori taught me the most useful lesson of my career: a child who chooses the work will go further than a child who is assigned it — so I plan from observation rather than from the page number we happen to be on. In practice that means short, clear input; a lot of speaking time that belongs to the students rather than to me; and mistakes treated as evidence of thinking rather than as something to correct out of existence. My job is to make the room feel safe enough that a child is willing to be wrong out loud in a second language.
